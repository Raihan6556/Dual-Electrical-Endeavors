Hello Yasin, there are many many units in electricity... It is okay if you do not understand the SI derivation or technical definitions yet, in a way that is intentional

| Unit | Symbol | Quantity | Fundamental SI / Derived Equivalent | Technical Definition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Volt** | V | Electric Potential / EMF | J/C (Joules per Coulomb) | The potential difference required to impart one joule of energy per coulomb of charge. Voltage is relative and needs.a reference point|
| **Ampere** | A | Electric Current | C/s (Coulombs per second) | The rate of flow of electric charge past a specific point. |
| **Ohm** | Ω | Electrical Resistance | V/A (Volts per Ampere) | The opposition to current flow when a potential difference of one volt produces a current of one ampere. |
| **Watt** | W | Electric Power | J/s (Joules per second), V·A | The rate at which electrical energy is transferred or dissipated. |
| **Coulomb** | C | Electric Charge | A·s (Ampere-seconds) | The total charge transported by a constant current of one ampere in one second. |
| **Farad** | F | Capacitance | C/V (Coulombs per Volt) | The storage capacity of an electrostatic field, where one volt of potential difference stores one coulomb of charge. |
| **Henry** | H | Inductance | V·s/A (Volt-seconds per Ampere) | The induction of one volt of electromotive force when the current changes at a rate of one ampere per second. |
| **Hertz** | Hz | Frequency | s^-1(Inverse seconds) | The number of complete alternating current (AC) cycles or periodic oscillations per second. |
